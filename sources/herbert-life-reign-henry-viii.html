Edward Herbert, Baron Herbert of Cherbury (1583–1648), The Life and Reign of King Henry the Eighth. London: Printed by Mary Clark for Ann Mearn, sold by Tho. Sawbridg, MDCLXXXIII (1683) — “Together with a General History of those Times.” ~38,254 lines concatenated from 672 OCR page files (Princeton University Library copy in the zip). Written under Charles I’s encouragement from the records; Herbert says the king whose history he writes is “subject to more obloquies than any since the worst Roman Emperors,” and that he will describe Henry “as He really was,” giving a favourable construction only where the case is doubtful. ^[source: herbert_life_reign_henry_viii_raw.txt]

Structure

Herbert writes a continuous reign history framed as year-by-year chronicle of Henry VIII (accession 22 April 1509 → death early 1547), with European “general history” woven in (France, Empire, papacy, Scotland). Navigation: use the year table below (first clear page-header occurrence of each year in the OCR). Where mid-reign headers conflict, prefer the thematic blocks that follow. ^[source: herbert_life_reign_henry_viii_raw.txt]

Contents — by phase

Dedication and opening portrait (lines ~54–330)

Epistle to the King: work begun by royal command, revised by the king’s eye, drawn from the records. Opening essay on the difficulty of writing a prince who changes; Henry’s education (once destined for Canterbury while Arthur lived); birth 28 June 1491; accession 22 April 1509; early virtues vs. later “self-will and cruelty.” ^[source: herbert_life_reign_henry_viii_raw.txt]

Early reign and French/Scottish wars (1509–1514; ~333–3280)

Accession settlement, Empson/Dudley fall as popular justice, marriage to Catherine of Aragon, early councils. French war, Spurs, Tournai; Flodden (1513) and Scottish aftermath; Wolsey rising in the diplomatic machine. ^[source: herbert_life_reign_henry_viii_raw.txt]

Wolsey zenith (1515–1525; ~3284–10000)

Wolsey as legate and chief councillor; European congress diplomacy; Field of Cloth of Gold (1520); Emperor Charles V; Amicable Grant stresses; Pavia (1525) and the shift of English alignments. ^[source: herbert_life_reign_henry_viii_raw.txt]

Divorce, fall of Wolsey, Reformation statutes (1526–1535; ~10024–24800)

Great Matter; legatine trial; Wolsey’s disgrace and death; Cranmer; Submission of the Clergy; Anne Boleyn’s rise and coronation; break with Rome and supremacy legislation; early execution politics (Fisher, More appear in this arc). ^[source: herbert_life_reign_henry_viii_raw.txt]

Queens, monasteries, Cromwell (1536–1540; ~24837–30500)

Anne’s fall (1536); Jane Seymour and the prince; Pilgrimage of Grace; dissolution accelerating; Six Articles climate; Cromwell’s foreign-marriage policy and fall (1540); Catherine Howard. ^[source: herbert_life_reign_henry_viii_raw.txt]

Late wars and death (1541–1547; ~30544–36690)

Scottish and French campaigns of the 1540s; Boulogne; Catherine Parr; the king’s decline and death (1547), with closing reflections on the reign. Index follows (~36691–end). ^[source: herbert_life_reign_henry_viii_raw.txt]
