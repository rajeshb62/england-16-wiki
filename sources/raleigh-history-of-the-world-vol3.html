Alexander Ross (1591–1654), The History of the World: The Second Part, in Six Books. London, 1652 (John Clark). This is not Sir Walter Ralegh’s own Book III. It is Ross’s continuation of Ralegh’s History, beginning where Ralegh stopped (end of the Macedonian kingdom / ~150 BC) and running to 1640/1641. Internet Archive files it as “Volume 3” under the same item that holds the 1736 Ralegh set — hence the KB filename raleigh_…_vol3_raw.txt. ~64,607 lines / ~3.1 MB plain text from DjVuTXT. ^[source: raleigh_history_of_the_world_vol3_raw.txt]

Internet Archive: item TheHistoryOfTheWorldInFiveBooks1736 — stream / DjVuTXT · download

Extractable? Yes — ready DjVuTXT; download only. Title-page OCR mangles the author name (aAL8XJSh…R^OSSl); imprint year is clear: M.DC.LII.

Companions: raleigh-history-of-the-world-vol1 / raleigh-history-of-the-world-vol2 are Ralegh’s own 1736 text (Life/Trial/Books I–V). For Tudor mining, this Ross volume is the valuable one: Book VI has continuous English/Scottish chapters from Lancastrians through Elizabeth, Mary Queen of Scots, James VI & I, and Charles I. ^[source: raleigh_history_of_the_world_vol3_raw.txt]

Quality Assessment

17th-century folio OCR (Google-digitized scan). Long-s → f is constant. Soft hyphens, broken words, and noisy running headers (Book VI., Hiftory of the World) are common. Chapter banners are often recoverable; some are OCR-garbled (Chap.ztf. for XXVI, Chap.2^. mid-block). Chronology pages are sparse/columnar. Alphabetical Table is usable for name lookup. Errata list after the first FINIS. ^[source: raleigh_history_of_the_world_vol3_raw.txt]

Best first stops for this KB: XXVII (H7–H8), XXXI (Ed6/Mary), XXXII (Elizabeth), XXXIII–XXXIV (Mary Queen of Scots / James VI). Cross-check denser narratives in bacon-history-henry-vii, herbert-life-reign-henry-viii, camden-annales-elizabeth. ^[source: raleigh_history_of_the_world_vol3_raw.txt]

Earlier Book VI chapters (I–XXIII) cover continental theatres (Holland, Helvetia, Sweden, Savoy, Germany, Italy, France, &c.) from ~1400 — useful background, not the main Tudor spine.
